Silva

a forest of things — a commonplace book arranged by affinity, not filing

Silva is a private place to keep passages, thoughts, questions and overheard lines — and, more importantly, a place that keeps noticing them for you. It never files anything for you and never explains what it finds. It only ever puts two things beside each other, once in a while, and lets you decide whether it's onto something.

Nursery — capture without commitment

Type or paste something worth keeping — that's almost the whole intake form, on purpose. No category, no tags, no kind, no clearing. It lands in the nursery, where it sits for a season (90 days by default). Each day, decide: Keep it into the forest, or Release it. Ignore it entirely and it simply fades out on its own once its season ends — no badge, no counter, no "284 items to process." A backlog that ages out instead of piling up.

One line sits beside the passage, and it's the single exception worth explaining: where did you encounter this? Unlike a clearing — which you can only recognise long afterwards — a source is the kind of context that evaporates. Months from now you'll still know why a line stopped you; you won't know which of four books you were holding when it did. So Silva asks while you still know, as plain text — Shelby Foote — The Civil War, or just a friend, on a walk — never a form to fill in. It stays entirely optional and never blocks a fast capture: leave it blank and nothing is lost that Silva could have kept for you. Behind the scenes that line becomes a real source the first time you write it, and is reused automatically whenever you name the same one again.

This delay is deliberate, not a missing feature. Ryan Holiday's own commonplace-book practice is the model: he waits weeks after finishing a book before transcribing highlights, because the wait pre-edits them — most of what felt urgent mid-read doesn't survive it. If you find yourself keeping almost everything the moment it lands, try leaving a batch alone for a few days first and see which of them you still reach for.

Four ways in, all landing in the same nursery:

  1. Type or paste — the everyday case: the words themselves, and optionally where you met them.
  2. Import from a Kobo e-reader — pick a KoboReader.sqlite file (copied off the device over USB) and Silva reads every highlight straight out of it. It proposes a source for each book and lets you confirm it, change it, or create a new one — Kobo's metadata differs between editions and re-downloads, so the match is a step you take rather than a guess Silva makes. Anything already imported is skipped, so re-dropping the same file every few weeks is the intended rhythm rather than a duplicate. Needs a computer and a cable, so it's the one lane that isn't phone-first — do it in a batch after finishing a book, not one highlight at a time.
  3. Share from anywhere — select text or a link in another app, share it to Silva (or, on iPhone, run a Shortcut), and the intake field opens already filled in, while you still remember why you shared it.
  4. Photograph a page — a picture of a physical book, kept as an Image thing. By default that's all it is: a photograph of a page is a legitimate thing to keep, not a stand-in for its text. Turn on Transcribe photographed pages automatically in the Hearth (needs an Anthropic key) and Silva pulls the text out for you the moment the photo lands — nothing to type. It's verbatim transcription only, never a description of the photo, and a plain photo with no legible text (a scene, an object) is simply left alone. Once a thing carries both, a small Photograph / Text toggle appears on its plate so you can read it either way, remembered per thing.

Forest — the kept collection

Every kept thing, one at a time, typeset like a specimen on a plate rather than a row in a table — meant to be read, not scanned. Tap Edit on anything to set its source (same plain text as at capture — attach one now if you skipped it, or correct one that's wrong), its locator (a page, a timestamp, "overheard on the 32 tram"), its Kind, a note, or a link — none of which you were forced to give at capture, all of which you can leave blank forever. A Suggest a kind button offers a guess (a question mark → Question, quoted speech → Dialogue, and so on) — only ever a starting point you can accept, change, or ignore; Silva never assigns a kind on its own.

The walk sits above the scroll: a short, finite stretch weighted toward what you haven't actually looked at in a while — not what's newest. Three things keep it from becoming a feed: it ends, and the ending offers nothing further, just a line saying it's done for now; it holds only your own kept things, nothing from outside; and it refreshes on the calendar, not on your attention — finishing it, or leaving it half-done, doesn't change tomorrow's walk, and reading something in it doesn't make it reshuffle out from under you.

It shows one thing at a time, with its position (2 of 5) beside it, and three ways out: Onward to the next, Back to the one before, and End the walk to stop wherever you are. On the last one Onward becomes Finish. Ending early and finishing are the same thing as far as Silva is concerned — neither is scored, neither is remembered as incomplete, and both leave you the same line saying the walk is done for now. It returns tomorrow, not sooner.

The full forest, in the order you kept things, is unchanged below it — every kept thing, one at a time, nothing filtered out. (Want just one clearing's things? That's what opening the clearing itself, from Clearings, is for — its own screen, with your own note on what you meant by it at the top, rather than a second version of this scroll.) If the walk isn't for you at all, it has a switch in the Hearth → What you see, and turning it off leaves the forest below untouched.

Underneath a thing's own text, a collapsed neighbourhood panel — open it to see what's near this one: paths you've already walked from here (with your own why alongside them), and any quiet oxblood threads the app has noticed nearby that you haven't connected yet. It's the same underground layer the graph shows, just centred on the one thing in front of you — present on a thing wherever it's read, whether that's in the walk or the plain scroll.

Kinds — what each one is for

A kind is a label you choose (or leave blank), never one Silva assigns. It exists so you can filter and skim by shape later — a book quote reads differently from a question you're still sitting with — not to classify anything for its own sake. When in doubt, leave it unset; an honest blank beats a wrong guess.

KindForExample
Passage Someone else's words, verbatim, with a source behind them. "The unexamined life is not worth living." — Plato, Apology
Observation Something you noticed, with no source to point to. Everyone on the platform checked their phone the instant the train stopped moving.
Dialogue Something someone said, quoted — overheard, remembered, or read. "You don't have to attend every argument you're invited to."
Question A question worth keeping unresolved, not one you're about to answer. What would I do differently if I expected to fail?
Fragment Too short to be a passage — a phrase, a title, a stray word that stopped you. "a controlled ruin"
Mine Something you wrote, not something you noticed — a composed thought, not a fleeting one. The line against Observation is soft; it's your call. A paragraph you drafted trying to work out why a decision felt wrong.
Link A URL worth keeping for what's behind it. A long-form piece you want to be able to find again by feel, not by search.
Image A photographed page — from the photo intake lane, or set on an existing thing later. A photo of a marked-up page from a library book you can't highlight.

Silva's own guess (Suggest a kind) uses simple, visible rules, not a model: a bare URL becomes Link, text ending in "?" becomes Question, a long quoted run becomes Dialogue, anything under 40 characters becomes Fragment, and everything else becomes Passage if it has a source or Observation if it doesn't. It never guesses Mine or Image — those are always your call.

Clearings — patterns you name

A clearing (Loci in Notion) is a pattern you noticed across a few kept things — coined after the fact, never assigned automatically or offered at capture. There is no "add to clearing" button on the intake form on purpose: a clearing has to already exist as a pattern in your head before it can exist in Silva.

A clearing usually starts from a provocation ("you have collected 11 things circling solitude") or from noticing it yourself while walking the forest. Once named, a clearing is a small collection with your own note on what you meant by it — write that note; it's what makes the clearing legible to you six months later, when the pattern that was obvious in the moment isn't anymore.

Clearings stay loose on purpose: combine two into one when they turn out to be the same idea under two names, or split a clearing's members into a new one when it turns out to be two patterns wearing one. Most things live in no clearing at all, permanently — that's not an unfinished state, it's most of the forest, always. A forest with 200 named clearings has none that mean anything; keep them few.

Paths — the connections you've made

Walk a path between two kept things and say why — the why is the point of the record, not the connection itself, and it isn't optional: Silva won't let you save a path, or edit one down, to an empty why. A path with no reason attached is exactly the kind of empty link-everything-to-everything graph this view exists to avoid. Every path you've walked, plus every kept thing, renders as an actual graph: clustered by clearing, connected by solid lines.

Look closely and you may also see faint, dashed oxblood threads — mycorrhiza, the underground layer this view is really about. These are things Silva noticed are quietly similar but you never explicitly connected — real, but unasserted. They're not clickable from the graph; the only way to act on one is if it surfaces as a provocation and you accept it, which is the one and only way a mycorrhizal thread becomes a real path. Nothing in Silva promotes one on its own. When you see that colour, you're looking at something the app noticed and you haven't yet — an invitation, not a fact.

The whole-forest graph is an overview; for the question you actually have day to day — what is this near? — use the neighbourhood panel on the thing itself (see Forest) instead of hunting for a node in the graph.

Forage — by meaning, not just words

One box does both plain text matching and semantic search — find "a canine waiting patiently" and it can surface "a dog sat at the tram stop," even with no words in common. The semantic side runs a small model entirely on your device (a one-time ~25 MB download, cached after); nothing you search is ever sent anywhere. Filter by Kind to browse a single facet — Questions only, Dialogue only — with or without a query.

Provocations — the app's only voice

At most once a session, Silva offers a small nudge: something forgotten, two things placed side by side, a clearing that might be quietly forming. It never explains why — you decide whether it's a real connection, and if you accept one, you write the Why yourself. Dismiss it and it won't come back; it stays quiet the rest of the session either way. It's also earned, not scheduled: the collection has to have actually changed or aged for one to appear, so opening Silva ten times in a day doesn't mean ten nudges.

Making it a habit

Silva has no onboarding flow and no checklist — on purpose, the same as the rest of the app. A few things that make it click faster, learned from how it's meant to be used rather than enforced in the UI:

Capture first, edit never (at first) Paste it and move on. Locator, kind, notes — all of that can wait until you're actually keeping the thing, days or weeks later. Treating intake as a two-second act is what makes it survive being interrupted mid-thought.
Let the season do the deciding Don't triage the nursery to zero. Things that were worth keeping resurface in your attention on their own; the ones that quietly expire were the ones you weren't going to keep anyway. Fighting the fade defeats its purpose.
Take the walk when it's there It's short by design — a few things, not a queue. Skipping it occasionally is fine; it isn't gone, it's just tomorrow's walk instead. But if you always skip it, it isn't doing its job of putting old things back in front of you.
Write the "why," even one line A path is only as useful later as its why is honest now. "Same idea, different century" is a complete answer. The connection without the reason is the thing this view exists to avoid becoming.
Name a clearing only once you can say what it means If you can't finish "this is about ___" in one sentence, it isn't a clearing yet — let a few more things land near it first.
Turn on "let Silva notice things" early, even before you trust it The one-time model download and the background indexing pass both take a little while on a fresh forest. Turning it on the day you set Silva up means it's already caught up by the time you'd want a mycorrhizal provocation.
Import Kobo highlights in batches, right after finishing a book It's the one lane that needs a computer and a cable — do it once per book rather than trying to make it a daily habit, and let the nursery's own delay do the pre-editing Ryan Holiday's practice relies on.
Turn things off in the Hearth rather than working around them The walk, the graph and provocations are each their own toggle under What's shown — nothing there changes what's kept, only what's visible. If the walk feels like one more thing to get through rather than an invitation, turn it off; the forest below it is unaffected either way. Same for the graph, and for provocations entirely — off just means the silence that's already the app's default state the rest of the time.

Silva is a herbarium sheet by daylight and the same cabinet at night — unbleached rag paper and green-black ink, or deep bark and aged ivory. the Hearth → Appearance follows your device by default; pick Daylight or Night there if you'd rather it didn't change under you at sunset. The choice applies to this guide too.

Some shapes people use Silva for, none exclusive of the others:

  • A reading commonplace book — the original use: passages from what you're reading, annotated with what they rhymed with, revisited by the walk instead of staying buried in the book you read them in.
  • A running observation log — things noticed in the world, not from books at all: what people said, what you overheard, small moments worth not losing. Mostly Observation and Dialogue.
  • A question drawer — genuine open questions you're not trying to resolve, kept so they resurface instead of being answered too quickly out of discomfort.
  • Material for your own writing — Mine things as drafted fragments and paragraphs, connected by path into an underground that becomes the outline once enough of it exists.
  • A slow-forming personal thesaurus of ideas — lean on clearings and let "near neighbours" provocations do the work of noticing repetition you wouldn't have connected yourself.

2 · Create an integration & connect

An internal integration is just a key that lets Silva reach your databases.

  1. Create it. Go to notion.so/my-integrations → New integration. Name it (e.g. Silva), pick the workspace your four databases live in, leave the type Internal, and save.
  2. Copy the token. On the integration's page, open Configuration, find the Internal Integration Secret, Show then Copy. Treat it like a password.
  3. Share all four databases with it. Open each one in Notion → top-right ••• → Connections → search your integration → select it. Without this, Silva sees nothing for that database.
  4. Paste the token into Silva. Open Silva → the Hearth → paste it into Notion token, then Test connection.

Your token (and, if you set one, your Anthropic key) is kept only in this browser and relayed to Notion/Anthropic through this site's proxy — never stored on a server.
