🎭 Guide

Marquee

The venues you actually go to, checked for new shows and tickets.

Marquee answers one question: did any of my venues put something new up? It reads the programme pages of a handful of places you chose, and tells you what changed since you last looked — a show that wasn’t there before, tickets that just went on sale, a night that just sold out.

It is not a discovery app and it does not have opinions. A venue’s own programme page is authoritative, so Marquee simply reads it, compares it to last time, and gets out of the way. There is no AI anywhere in the pipeline and no web search — which is also why it costs essentially nothing to run.

Marquee vs Radar-B. Radar-B asks “what’s happening in this city?” and draws on eight publications through the /recommend in Bucharest skill. Marquee asks “what’s on at my places?” and reads those venues directly. Different question, different machinery. Both hand anything you keep to the same place: Wanderlist.

01Checking your venues

Press Check venues. Marquee fetches every active venue’s programme page one at a time, reads the showings out of it, and shows you the result. A check of seven venues takes something like ten to thirty seconds, mostly waiting on the venues’ own servers.

Nothing happens on a schedule. Marquee checks when you ask it to, and remembers the last check so reopening the app shows you the programme without fetching anything.

The first check is a baseline, not news. With nothing to compare against, every single showing is technically “new” — a hundred of them tells you nothing. So the first check says so and stays quiet. From the second check on, the strip at the top is only what moved.

02Reading “what changed”

This strip is the point of the app. There are four kinds of change:

ChangeMeans
tickets on sale A show that was listed without a buy option now has one. This is the one worth acting on today — it is the whole reason to check a venue rather than wait for its newsletter.
new A showing that wasn’t on the page last time. New productions, and new dates for a run you already knew about.
sold out A night that had tickets and no longer does.
gone from the programme A future showing the venue has stopped listing. Usually a cancellation — occasionally just a page being reorganised.

They are ordered by what you can do about them: things on sale first, then things gone, then things newly listed.

When nothing moved, the strip says “Nothing new since Tuesday.” That is a real answer and the commonest one — the app is meant to be checked in ten seconds and closed.

03Reading the programme

Below the strip is everything currently listed, grouped by day. Each card is a production — a title at a venue — not a single showing, because a cinema listing the same film six times should be one card, not six. Its dates sit underneath it.

  • tickets — at least one date can be bought.
  • sold out — every date is gone, not just one.
  • in Wanderlist — you kept it.
  • A price chip appears only when the venue published one. Marquee never guesses a price.

Individual dates are clickable: each one keeps that specific night. A date with a line through it is sold out — you can still keep it, in case a return shows up.

Ignore silences a production permanently, including its future dates. Ignoring a film you have no interest in means never seeing it again, not seeing it again on Thursday. The venue filter chips narrow the list to one place.

04Keeping a night

Marquee has no saved list of its own. Press Keep and the showing becomes a row in Wanderlist’s Findings database, which is where all your things-to-do already live. Two backlogs would be one too many.

The sheet opens as an editable draft, already filled in with:

  • Category from that venue’s default — play for a theatre, movie for a cinema, concert for a hall.
  • Place as venue plus street plus city, because a bare venue name doesn’t geocode and the map pin comes out wrong.
  • Planned date and time from the showing, and Date Expiring set to the same date — a one-off night is the case where missing it means missing it.
  • Cost, only when the venue published a price.
  • Link — the ticket URL when there is one, otherwise the show’s own page.

Nothing is ever saved as “Going”. Keeping something means it is worth considering, not that you have committed to it. Set Going yourself, in Wanderlist, when you have actually decided.

What if it’s already in Wanderlist?

Marquee reads your Findings database when it starts, so it knows what you already have — including rows you added from Wanderlist itself, or from another device. A production with something kept shows a chip saying so, and the exact dates you kept are ticked in its date list.

Try to keep the same night twice and the sheet stops you: it says the night is already there (and whether you marked it going or attended) and offers Keep a second copy instead of the usual save. A second copy is occasionally what you want; it just shouldn’t happen by accident.

A different date of a run you already kept is not a duplicate — it says a sibling exists and saves normally. Neither is the same show at a different venue.

Matching is by name, venue and date, with accents and capitalisation ignored. If you rename a row in Wanderlist beyond recognition, Marquee stops seeing it as the same thing and will offer to keep it again.

05Adding, pausing, removing venues

The Venues tab is the list of places being read. Four things you can do:

Add

Paste the venue’s programme page URL — the listing, not the homepage. Marquee works out which reader handles that site from the URL itself and fills in the name for you. It tells you straight away whether it can read the site, before you commit.

Pause

A paused venue keeps its row, its notes and its place in the list, sits dimmed at the bottom, and is simply not read on a check. Use it for a club that goes quiet over the summer. It is as easy to undo as it was to do.

Edit

Name, category, area, address, notes. Editing a venue never touches its check history.

Remove

Removing archives the venue’s Notion row rather than deleting it, so Notion’s trash is your undo. If you only want to stop checking somewhere, pause it instead.

Not every site can be added by pasting a URL. Marquee ships readers for Teatrul Excelsior, Eventbook (which covers four cinemas and clubs at once), Filarmonica George Enescu, and any page publishing standard schema.org event data — which is how Expirat works.

A venue outside that list gets the generic reader, and the form says plainly that it may find nothing. Sites that publish structured event data usually just work; the rest need a small reader written for them, which is a code change, not something the app can do for you.

06When a venue can’t be read

A scraper that quietly returns nothing is worse than one that breaks loudly, because “this venue has nothing on” and “this venue’s website changed” look identical from the outside. Marquee refuses to conflate them. Every reader knows roughly what that venue normally lists, and a page that loads but yields nothing is reported, in a strip above the programme, as a problem:

SaysMeansWhat to do
markup has probably changed The page loaded but nothing could be read out of it, or far less than usual. The reader needs updating — a code change. The venue is fine; Marquee isn’t.
rate-limiting us The venue’s server refused this request after answering earlier ones. Not a breakage. Wait a few minutes and check again. Nothing is wrong with the venue or the reader.
answered 404 / could not reach The URL is wrong, or the site is down. Open the URL yourself; fix it in the venue’s settings if the page moved.
nothing upcoming is listed Read fine, genuinely empty — between seasons, or the programme hasn’t been published yet. Nothing. This one is real.

A venue that failed to answer never has its events reported as cancelled, and its last-known programme is kept rather than wiped. One bad request should not empty the app and then refill it next time as if everything were brand new.

07Settings

Settings, in the top bar, holds everything that isn't a venue or a showing.

Appearance

System, Light or Dark. System is the default and follows your device — including when it flips at sunset, which a fixed choice never would.

Programme

  • Hide sold-out productions — hides runs where every date is gone. A production with one night still on sale is never hidden.
  • Show what you've ignored — brings ignored productions back into the list so you can un-ignore them. Ignoring is otherwise permanent, so this is how you undo it.
  • Keep the day's showings after they start — off by default, because a 19:00 concert stops being useful at 19:30. Turn it on if you'd rather see the whole day regardless.

08Connecting Notion

Without a token, Marquee runs in demo mode: seven real Bucharest venues held in memory. Checking them genuinely reads their pages, so you can see exactly what the app does — but venue edits and anything you keep never reach Notion.

To connect it properly, open Settings → Notion and paste:

  • Your Notion integration token. It is stored in this browser only, and travels no further than the relay that talks to Notion on your behalf.
  • The Marquee — Watched Venues database (under Dev → App Databases). It is pre-filled; you only need to change it if you are starting from your own copy.

Test connection checks it without changing anything. The integration also needs access to your Wanderlist Findings database for keeping to work.

09Honest limits

  • It only checks when you press the button. Nothing runs while the app is closed, so tickets can go on sale and sell out between two checks without Marquee ever seeing it. A scheduled check with an email is possible later; it isn’t built.
  • Readers are hand-written and sites change. Four sites is maintainable and the health check makes breakage visible, but a redesign will eventually need a code change.
  • “Gone from the programme” is inference, not fact. The venue stopped listing it; that is usually a cancellation and occasionally a reorganised page.
  • Prices and times are only ever repeated, never guessed. A missing price means the venue didn’t publish one.
  • What you ignored is per-browser — along with your theme and view toggles. What you kept lives in Notion and is read back, so it follows you everywhere.
