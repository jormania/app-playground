🎭 Guide

Marquee

The venues you actually go to, checked for new shows and tickets.

Marquee answers one question: did any of my venues put something new up? It reads the programme pages of a handful of places you chose, and tells you what changed since you last looked — a show that wasn’t there before, tickets that just went on sale, a night that just sold out.

It is not a discovery app and it does not have opinions. A venue’s own programme page is authoritative, so Marquee simply reads it, compares it to last time, and gets out of the way. There is no AI anywhere in the pipeline and no web search — which is also why it costs essentially nothing to run.

Marquee vs Radar-B. Radar-B asks “what’s happening in this city?” and draws on eight publications through the /recommend in Bucharest skill. Marquee asks “what’s on at my places?” and reads those venues directly. Different question, different machinery. Both hand anything you keep to the same place: Wanderlist.

01Checking your venues

Press Check venues. Marquee fetches every active venue’s programme page one at a time, reads the showings out of it, and shows you the result — a handful of venues takes something like ten to thirty seconds, mostly waiting on the venues’ own servers.

Nothing happens on a schedule. Marquee checks when you ask it to, and remembers the last check so reopening the app shows you the programme without fetching anything.

The first check is a baseline, not news. With nothing to compare against, every single showing is technically “new” — a hundred of them tells you nothing. So the first check says so and stays quiet. From the second check on, the strip at the top is only what moved.

02Reading “what changed”

This strip is the point of the app. There are four kinds of change:

ChangeMeans
tickets on sale A show that was listed without a buy option now has one. This is the one worth acting on today — it is the whole reason to check a venue rather than wait for its newsletter.
new A showing that wasn’t on the page last time. New productions, and new dates for a run you already knew about.
sold out A night that had tickets and no longer does.
gone from the programme A future showing the venue has stopped listing. Usually a cancellation — occasionally just a page being reorganised.

They are ordered by what you can do about them: things on sale first, then things gone, then things newly listed.

When nothing moved, the strip says “Nothing new since Tuesday.” That is a real answer and the commonest one — the app is meant to be checked in ten seconds and closed.

Tap a row to jump straight to the card it's about, scrolled into view and briefly outlined. The × in the corner dismisses exactly what's currently showing — it doesn't delete anything, and it stays dismissed across however many checks it takes before something genuinely new turns up. A check that finds the same nothing-new situation, or turns up only things you already dismissed, stays quiet; the moment a check finds something that wasn't already dismissed, the strip reappears with just that.

03Reading the programme

Below the strip is everything currently listed, grouped by day. Each card is a production — a title at a venue — not a single showing, because a cinema listing the same film six times should be one card, not six. Its dates sit underneath it, and a poster appears wherever the venue published one — not every reader has one to give.

  • tickets — at least one date can be bought.
  • sold out — every date is gone, not just one.
  • in Wanderlist — you kept it.
  • A price chip appears only when the venue published one. Marquee never guesses a price.

Individual dates are clickable: each one keeps that specific night. A date with a line through it is sold out, and can't be kept — there's nothing left to keep. A date carrying its own colour (matching a row in the strip above) is one this check specifically found changed — a run where one night just opened and another just sold out shows both, right on the dates they happened to.

With enough venues watched, the list grows past what's easy to scan by eye — the search box on the tabs row matches a title or a venue as you type, diacritics and case ignored, so "sperante" finds "speranțe". The same box sits on the Venues tab, where it searches your venues instead — by name, area, address or site. Each tab remembers its own query, so searching one never quietly empties the other.

A festival shows as its individual nights, not as itself. Venues routinely list a festival three ways at once — one entry per day, one for the whole run, and a season pass. Marquee keeps the days you can actually go to and drops the summary, which otherwise sorts to the top of the first day and duplicates it. A multi-day event whose individual days are not listed separately is kept as it is, because then it really is one thing.

Ignore silences a production permanently, including its future dates. Ignoring a film you have no interest in means never seeing it again, not seeing it again on Thursday.

On a touch device, both are also a swipe on the card itself: right opens Keep for its first date, left is Ignore — the two buttons under a different finger, not a third action. Keeps the same "which date is that ambiguous mid-swipe" answer the Keep button already gives: whichever date shows first, editable in the sheet that opens. Turn it off in Settings if a card full of dates makes an accidental swipe too easy; the buttons keep working either way.

The two icons beside Check venues, top right: the middle one switches between this list and a poster wall — the same productions as a wall of covers instead of rows, for the days a face is faster to recognise than a title. Same Keep and Ignore, same colours for what's changed; just bigger posters and less text. Your choice is remembered.

Above the strip, a row of seven days — today first — shades by how much is on each one: a quiet Tuesday looks quiet, a busy Saturday looks busy. Tap a day with something on it to jump straight there.

The filter chips narrow the list in up to three steps: a category row (Theatre, Cinema, Concert…) appears once more than one is actually in play, picking one reveals just that category's venues, and a venue with more than one hall — the National Theatre's seven, for instance — gets its own hall row once it's selected. A single-category setup skips straight to a flat venue row, same as before.

Cinemas only look 10 days out; everything else looks 120. A working cinema publishes weeks of showings nobody actually plans a trip to the pictures around that far in advance, so any venue whose category is a cinema is limited automatically — nothing to turn on, and it applies to the next one you add too. A theatre or concert hall keeps the long view.

04Keeping a night

Marquee has no saved list of its own. Press Keep and the showing becomes a row in Wanderlist’s Findings database, which is where all your things-to-do already live. Two backlogs would be one too many.

The sheet opens as an editable draft, already filled in with:

  • Description, leading with the venue’s own words when a reader found any — most do — ahead of the facts Marquee itself can add (dates, hall, price). Never shown anywhere but here: the programme itself stays title, venue and dates, nothing more.
  • Category from that venue’s default — play for a theatre, movie for a cinema, concert for a hall.
  • Place as venue plus street plus city, because a bare venue name doesn’t geocode and the map pin comes out wrong.
  • Planned date and time from the showing, and Date Expiring set to the same date — a one-off night is the case where missing it means missing it.
  • Cost, only when the venue published a price — left blank rather than guessed otherwise.
  • Link — the ticket URL when there is one, otherwise the show’s own page.

Saving shows a brief confirmation with an Undo — press it in the next few seconds and the row is removed from Wanderlist again, no different from deleting it there yourself. After that, Wanderlist is where you'd go to change your mind.

Nothing is ever saved as “Going”. Keeping something means it is worth considering, not that you have committed to it. Set Going yourself, in Wanderlist, when you have actually decided.

What if it’s already in Wanderlist?

Marquee reads your Findings database when it starts, so it knows what you already have — including rows you added from Wanderlist itself, or from another device. A production with something kept shows a chip saying so, and the exact dates you kept are ticked in its date list.

Try to keep the same night twice and the sheet stops you: it says the night is already there (and whether you marked it going or attended) and offers Keep a second copy instead of the usual save. A second copy is occasionally what you want; it just shouldn’t happen by accident.

A different date of a run you already kept is not a duplicate — it says a sibling exists and saves normally. Neither is the same show at a different venue.

Matching is by name, venue and date, with accents and capitalisation ignored. If you rename a row in Wanderlist beyond recognition, Marquee stops seeing it as the same thing and will offer to keep it again.

05Adding, pausing, removing venues

The Venues tab is the list of places being read. Four things you can do:

Add

Paste the venue’s programme page URL — the listing, not the homepage. Marquee works out which reader handles that site from the URL itself and fills in the name for you. It tells you straight away whether it can read the site, before you commit.

Each row also carries its own health: which reader handles it, when it was last checked — including a check that ran overnight while the app was closed — and what that check found. A venue the last check couldn't read is marked not readable (or rate-limited, which is a venue's server being busy, not a breakage), with the reason on the row rather than buried in the same grey as a successful count.

Pause

A paused venue keeps its row, its notes and its place in the list, sits dimmed at the bottom, and is simply not read on a check. Use it for a club that goes quiet over the summer. It is as easy to undo as it was to do.

Edit

Name, category, area, address, notes. Editing a venue never touches its check history.

Remove

Removing archives the venue’s Notion row rather than deleting it, so Notion’s trash is your undo. If you only want to stop checking somewhere, pause it instead.

Moving a venue to a different source

Some venues are easier to read through their ticketing platform than through their own site. Paste the new URL over the old one and the reader is re-resolved from it — the form always tells you which reader will handle the address currently in the box, and that is what gets saved.

Changing a venue does not re-check it. The programme you are looking at is always the last check, so it will say it is out of date until you press Check venues again — including any problem it reported.

Not every site can be added by pasting a URL. Marquee ships a handful of readers, each built for one site or one ticketing platform — some cover several venues at once when they share a platform, and a venue with several halls of its own can be read as one row rather than one per hall. Anything publishing standard schema.org event data works out of the box, with no reader written specifically for it.

A venue outside all of that gets the generic reader, and the form says plainly that it may find nothing. Sites that publish structured event data usually just work; the rest need a small reader written for them, which is a code change, not something the app can do for you — check the form's own message when you paste a URL rather than assuming.

06When a venue can’t be read

A scraper that quietly returns nothing is worse than one that breaks loudly, because “this venue has nothing on” and “this venue’s website changed” look identical from the outside. Marquee refuses to conflate them. Every reader knows roughly what that venue normally lists, and a page that loads but yields nothing is reported, in a strip above the programme, as a problem:

SaysMeansWhat to do
markup has probably changed The page loaded but nothing could be read out of it, or far less than usual. The reader needs updating — a code change. The venue is fine; Marquee isn’t.
rate-limiting us The venue’s server refused this request after answering earlier ones. Not a breakage. Wait a few minutes and check again. Nothing is wrong with the venue or the reader.
answered 404 / could not reach The URL is wrong, or the site is down. Open the URL yourself; fix it in the venue’s settings if the page moved.
nothing upcoming is listed Read fine, genuinely empty — between seasons, or the programme hasn’t been published yet. Nothing. This one is real.

A venue that failed to answer never has its events reported as cancelled, and its last-known programme is kept rather than wiped. One bad request should not empty the app and then refill it next time as if everything were brand new.

07Settings

Settings, in the top bar, holds everything that isn't a venue or a showing.

Appearance

System, Light or Dark. System is the default and follows your device — including when it flips at sunset, which a fixed choice never would.

Programme

  • Hide sold-out productions — hides runs where every date is gone. A production with one night still on sale is never hidden.
  • Show what you've ignored — brings ignored productions back into the list so you can un-ignore them. Ignoring is otherwise permanent, so this is how you undo it.
  • Keep the day's showings after they start — off by default, because a 19:00 concert stops being useful at 19:30. Turn it on if you'd rather see the whole day regardless.
  • Hide what's already in Wanderlist — hides a production once every date of its run is already kept there, the same "only when all of it" rule sold-out uses. A run with one night kept and others still undecided stays visible.
  • Swipe to Keep or Ignore — on by default. Turn it off to use the Keep and Ignore buttons only, if a card full of dates makes an accidental swipe too easy.
  • Compact list — a smaller cover and tighter rows, for fitting more of the programme on screen at once. The poster wall keeps its own size either way.

Notify

Opt in and Marquee checks your venues in the background, even while it's closed, and lets you know when tickets open — the one change actually worth a nudge for. Off by default; turning it on asks your browser for permission.

  • Also notify about new listings and sold-out — off by default. Tickets opening is the one you'd want to know about right away; the other two are more background noise than urgency.
  • Quiet hours (11pm–8am) — only shown once notifications are on. Holds anything that happens overnight and delivers it as one check the next morning instead of a 2am ping.

Background checking needs the app installed, on Chromium — desktop or Android. iOS Safari and an ordinary browser tab fall back to checking only while the app is open, and Settings says so rather than offering a toggle that can't work there. Even where it's supported, the browser decides exactly when to wake it — the interval Marquee asks for is a floor, not a promise.

Seven quick taps on the small print under Notify reveals a diagnostics panel — permission state, whether background checking is actually registered, and what the last background check found — for the rare case one goes quiet and you want to know why.

08Connecting Notion

Without a token, Marquee runs in demo mode: real Bucharest venues held in memory. Checking them genuinely reads their pages, so you can see exactly what the app does — but venue edits and anything you keep never reach Notion.

To connect it properly, open Settings → Notion and paste:

  • Your Notion integration token. It is stored in this browser only, and travels no further than the relay that talks to Notion on your behalf.
  • The Marquee — Watched Venues database (under Dev → App Databases). It is pre-filled; you only need to change it if you are starting from your own copy.

Test connection checks both databases separately and tells you which one answered, because they fail separately and mean different things.

Your integration needs access to two databases, not one. Sharing it with Marquee — Watched Venues is easy to do and easy to stop there; but Findings is where keeping writes, so without it every save fails and Marquee can't tell what you already have.

In Notion, open the Findings database → ••• (top right) → Connections → add your integration. Then press Test connection again.

09Honest limits

  • Two separate ways a check can happen without you pressing the button — a nightly one that rides on Wanderlist's own reminder email (needs the server's own Notion access configured, not a Marquee setting at all), and the Notify toggle's own background check on your device. Neither is guaranteed: without the server-side one set up, and with Notify off or unsupported, Marquee only checks when you press the button, and nothing runs while the app is closed.
  • Readers are hand-written and sites change. A redesign will eventually need a code change; the health check (§6) is what makes that breakage visible rather than silent.
  • “Gone from the programme” is inference, not fact. The venue stopped listing it; that is usually a cancellation and occasionally a reorganised page.
  • Prices and times are only ever repeated, never guessed. A missing price means the venue didn’t publish one.
  • What you ignored is per-browser — along with your theme and view toggles. What you kept lives in Notion and is read back, so it follows you everywhere.
