1. What it is

WhereItWent answers one question well: where did the money actually go? You record what you spend and earn, and it turns that into budgets, trends, and a readable narrative about the month.

The unusual part is the storage. There is no WhereItWent server and no account to create. Every transaction lives in Notion databases you own, reached with your own integration token, which never leaves your device. Cancel any time by deleting the token — the data stays where it always was, in Notion, queryable and exportable by you.

What it does well

  • Budgets that match real life — monthly, quarterly or yearly, with unspent room carried forward
  • Foreign spending recorded at the real rate, with travel grouped by trip
  • A narrative read on the month, not just charts
  • Recurring bills posted automatically and warned about beforehand

What it deliberately isn't

  • No bank connection — nothing is scraped or synced from a bank
  • No account balances or net worth (see §14)
  • No shared or household access; it is single-person by design
  • Not a receipt store — Notion is better at that than a form field

2. Try it without setting anything up

Open the app with no configuration and it runs on a full year of realistic sample data — hundreds of transactions, budgets, subscriptions and trips. A bar across the top says Sample data so it can never be mistaken for your own.

Everything works in this mode: add, edit, delete, merge duplicates, change budgets. Nothing is written to Notion, and a reload restores the sample set. It is the right place to decide whether the app suits you before creating a single database.

To leave sample mode, connect Notion (§3). To go back to it, empty the token field in Settings and save, or use Erase Notion data & use samples — which is destructive, so read §12 first.

3. Connecting your Notion

  1. Create the five databases in Notion, with the exact property names in §4. Full-page databases anywhere in your workspace.
  2. Create an integration at notion.so/my-integrations and copy the Internal Integration Secret (it starts ntn_).
  3. Share each database with it. Open the database, ••• → Connections → add your integration. Miss one and that section of the app stays empty.
  4. Paste it all into Settings. The token, then a link or ID for each database. Pasting the whole Notion URL is fine — the ID is extracted for you.
  5. Save. Every database you filled in is queried before the settings are accepted, so a typo is caught immediately rather than surfacing later as an empty dashboard.

Only Categories, Accounts and Transactions are required. Subscriptions and Trips are optional — leave them blank and those features simply stay quiet.

Your token is kept in this browser's local storage and sent only to Notion, through a thin proxy that adds no storage of its own. Use the app on another device and you enter the token again there; nothing syncs between devices except through Notion itself.

5. Recording money

Everything starts at + Add in the header. The form adapts to the type you pick, and remembers whichever type you added last — so a freelancer logging mostly Income doesn't reselect it every time.

Expense and Income

Date, amount, description, category, account. Choosing a category suggests an account based on how that kind of money usually moves — salary to the current account, rent and gifts to cash, everyday spending to the card you actually use. It is only ever a suggestion, and it never fires when you are editing an existing transaction: an account already recorded is a fact about where money went, and correcting a category must not quietly move it.

Repeat

Every row in the ledger has a small ↻ next to its amount. Tap it and Add reopens pre-filled with that transaction's description, amount, category and account — the same coffee, the same parking fare, without retyping it. The date resets to today and notes start blank, since both are specific to the moment, not the purchase.

Transfers off by default

Turn on Transfers in Settings to record money moving between your own accounts — cash into Revolut, current account into savings. A transfer names both ends, From and To, and they must differ.

Transfers have no category and are excluded from every income and expense total, every budget and every Insights figure. They exist so the ledger reflects reality without inflating what you appear to earn or spend. In the ledger they show as Cash → Revolut with a ± amount.

Editing an existing transfer keeps working even if you later switch the feature off, so disabling it can never silently reclassify old data.

Editing and deleting

Tap any row in the ledger or on the Dashboard to open it. Deleting archives the Notion page rather than destroying it — recoverable from Notion's trash for 30 days. The app itself has no undo.

6. Foreign currency & travel

Give an account or a trip a currency other than RON and the amount field lets you type what you actually paid. The RON figure is filled in underneath from the European Central Bank's rate for that date, with the rate spelled out: Rate: 1 EUR = 5.2353 RON (29 Jul).

  • RON stays the source of truth. Every total, budget and chart is built from it. The foreign amount is recorded alongside as context.
  • You can override the RON figure. Type over it and it stops following the rate — a card's own fee beats any published number. The displayed rate then reflects what you actually got.
  • Historical rates are used for back-dated transactions. Ask for a weekend and you get the preceding business day, with that date shown.
  • A trip's currency beats the account's. On a trip you spend the destination's money whatever card settles it.
  • Rates are cached per day, so a currency is fetched once. Offline, the last known rate is used and labelled as such.
  • BGN can be recorded but not converted — Bulgaria adopted the euro and the ECB no longer quotes it. Enter the RON amount yourself.
  • The currency picker floats what you need to the top: RON first, then the account's or trip's own currency, then whatever you've used recently — instead of one flat 16-item list scrolled the same way regardless of which two or three currencies you actually use.

Assign travel spending to a Trip and Insights totals it separately — flights, hotels and on-the-ground costs — including a per-currency breakdown so the trip reads in the money you actually handled. Editing a transaction that belongs to a trip shows a "View this trip in Insights" link, jumping straight to that trip's breakdown instead of picking it manually from the filter afterwards.

7. Budgets, periods & rollover on by default

Give a category a limit and choose how long that limit lasts: per month, per quarter or per year. Annual costs like insurance or taxes no longer have to be faked as a twelfth each month.

Each bar is measured against its own window and is deliberately unaffected by the period picker and the filters — otherwise a monthly cap viewed as "This year" would read as permanently blown. The window is always labelled (Jul 2026, Q3 2026, 2026), because a yearly budget at 40% must not be mistaken for 40% of this month.

Set Budget Anchor to move a quarterly or yearly window off the calendar — a March anchor makes the year run March to February.

The Dashboard's Total Global Budget bar sums every category's limit and spend — but a monthly and a yearly category added straight together would mix timescales into a meaningless number, so each category's share is first normalized to its monthly equivalent (a yearly limit contributes 1/12th). The bar labels itself "(per month)" whenever a non-monthly budget is in the mix.

Rollover

Tick Roll unspent room over and whatever you did not spend is added to the next window. Overspend carries too, as a negative — money already spent cannot be un-spent, and forgiving it each period would quietly hide the hole.

Carry is recalculated from your transaction history every time, never stored. So correcting a transaction from three months ago fixes every window after it automatically, and there is no second copy of the number to fall out of step.

8. Recurring bills & reminders on by default

Add anything charged on the same day each month under Settings → Recurring Subscriptions. Two things then happen.

It posts the charge

On launch, any charge whose day has arrived is written into the ledger, tagged Subscription and Auto-generated. Before writing, the ledger is checked for a matching charge that month, so a failed run or a second device cannot double-post. Backfill is capped at 12 months.

It warns you first

A Next 30 Days agenda on the Dashboard lists what is coming, with a net total; a charge you already entered by hand is greyed out as "already recorded". A slim bar at the top names anything inside your lead time (default 5 days, configurable). That bar cannot be dismissed on purpose — silencing an unpaid bill hides the one thing worth being told about. It clears itself when the charge lands.

Notifications while the app is closed opt-in

Settings → Bill Reminders turns on a device notification a few days before a charge. It is entirely on-device — no server, no push service, nothing sent anywhere.

Background delivery needs the app installed (see §13) on Chromium — desktop or Android. iOS Safari and ordinary browser tabs fall back to the in-app bar, and the settings panel says so rather than offering a switch that cannot work. Even where supported, the browser decides when to wake the app: the lead time is a floor, not a promise. Seven taps on the Bill Reminders heading reveals a diagnostics panel for when a reminder goes quiet.

9. Insights & the 90-day forecast

The Insights tab is organised by what you can do about it.

Act — attention & wins
A written summary of the period, then ranked alerts: a category well above its own multi-month average, spending pace that outruns income, an unusually large single purchase. Alerts need at least two months of real history before they fire, and are capped so the panel stays readable. Wins are called out too. A Copy summary button beneath the write-up copies it, plus income/expenses/net/savings rate, as plain text — for pasting into a chat or a note without a screenshot.
Ahead — the next 90 days follows the Cash flow toggle
Per month: committed (scheduled subscriptions, near-certain) and estimated (the trailing three-month median of everything else) drawn as separate segments, so a guess never borrows the authority of a known charge. A median, not an average, so one holiday does not poison three months. Under three complete months of history it says so and estimates nothing.
It projects cash flow, not a balance — the app tracks what you spend, not what you hold, and inventing an opening balance would be worse than the gap.
Understand — the architecture
Savings rate as the headline, fixed versus discretionary costs, a 50/30/20 read, and where income comes from. Money moved into savings or investments is excluded from spending, so investing never lowers your reported savings rate. Anything the classifier could not place is counted but flagged rather than silently filed as a "want".
Explore — patterns
Category trends against equivalent past periods, frequent vendors, largest transactions, and the Travel / Property / family dashboards.

Every comparison is like-for-like: a month in progress is compared against the same number of days of the previous month, not against a complete one.

10. Duplicate detection

When the same charge is recorded twice — a failed sync, two devices, a manual entry that the recurring engine also posted — a review card appears at the top of the ledger, and the Transactions tab in the header carries a small dot so it's discoverable without already being there.

It is deliberately cautious, because a false positive invites you to delete something real. A pair must match to the cent, sit within a day of each other, share a category, and have near-identical descriptions. Beyond that:

  • Habitual charges are left alone. If the same vendor and amount appears on three or more separate days it is a fixed price — the same coffee, the same fare — where a matching amount means nothing. A vendor whose charges vary landing on the identical figure twice is far more telling. This is measured from your own history, not from a list of guessed vendor names.
  • Same day, same vendor, same card is the only combination rated high. Anything crossing midnight stays a suggestion.
  • Two foreign charges must also agree on the original amount and currency, not just the converted RON figure.

Tap either row to open it in full before deciding. Choosing Keep this copies over any notes, trip or tags the other row has and that this one lacks, then archives the rest — behind a confirmation. Not duplicates is remembered on this device.

11. Working offline

Every successful load is mirrored locally, so the app opens instantly and keeps working without a connection.

  • Reading: if a refresh fails, the ledger you already have is shown behind a bar reading Showing data from 14:32 — offline. It never pretends to be current.
  • Writing: changes are saved on the device and queued. They appear in the ledger immediately, marked ⏳ Syncing, and are sent in order when the connection returns.
  • Order is preserved. If one change cannot be sent, everything after it waits rather than jumping the queue — reordering writes could resurrect a row you deleted.
  • Nothing is dropped silently. Anything Notion actively rejects is listed under Settings → Changes Notion rejected with the real error, and Retry / Discard.

While offline, or while changes are queued, automatic subscription posting pauses. It decides what to post by checking the ledger, and a ledger that predates your queued changes would make it post them all a second time.

12. Settings reference

Connection Details, Feature Toggles, Recurring Subscriptions and Trips Management are each collapsible — everything starts open, but collapsing one is remembered on this device, so a long list of subscriptions or trips can be put away for good rather than scrolled past every visit.

Feature toggles

Budget limits on
The limits panel and progress bars on the Dashboard.
Cash flow on
The income-vs-expenses chart, and the 90-day projection in Insights.
Transfers off
The third transaction type. Most people never need to track internal moves, so it stays invisible until asked for.
Upcoming bills on
The Dashboard agenda and the reminder bar. Turning this off also turns off background notifications.

Managing data

Recurring Subscriptions and Trips Management let you add, edit and remove those records without leaving the app. Deleting archives in Notion.

Erase Notion data & use samples archives every Transaction, Subscription and Trip in your real workspace and switches the app to sample data. You must type a confirmation word. Notion keeps archived pages in its trash for 30 days, so they can be restored from there — but WhereItWent cannot undo it. This is for starting genuinely clean, not for tidying up.

To disconnect without deleting anything, empty the token and database fields and save. The app returns to sample data and your Notion is untouched.

Appearance

A sun/moon button switches light and dark. A brand new install defaults to whatever your device's own light/dark setting is, rather than always opening dark; once you choose explicitly, that choice is remembered on the device and the guide follows it.

13. Installing as an app

WhereItWent is a Progressive Web App. Installing it gives a home-screen icon, a window without browser chrome, and — on Chromium — the ability to deliver reminders in the background.

  • Desktop Chrome / Edge: the install icon in the address bar, or ⋮ → Install.
  • Android Chrome: ⋮ → Add to Home screen / Install app.
  • iOS Safari: Share → Add to Home Screen. Works fully except background notifications, which iOS does not offer to web apps.

Launch it from the installed icon rather than a tab — Chromium only grants background wake-ups to installed apps, and it weighs how often you actually use it.

14. Honest limits

Things it does not do, stated plainly so you are not left hunting for them:

  • No account balances or net worth. The app records movement, not holdings — which is why the forecast projects cash flow rather than a balance.
  • No bank import. Every row is entered by hand, or posted by the recurring engine.
  • No CSV import or export from the app. Notion does both, and your data is already there.
  • No split transactions. A shop that was half groceries, half household needs two rows.
  • No savings goals, no receipt attachments, no tag editing in the form, no in-app undo.
  • No household or shared access. Deliberately single-person; share the Notion databases if someone else needs to see them.
  • One base currency, RON. Foreign amounts are recorded and converted, but every total is in RON.
  • No cross-device sync of preferences. Token, theme, lead time and dismissals are per-device. The financial data syncs through Notion.

15. Troubleshooting & FAQ

I connected Notion but a section is empty.
Almost always a database that was not shared with the integration. Open it in Notion, ••• → Connections, add the integration. Saving in Settings queries every database you filled in, so re-saving will tell you which one fails.
Budgets show nothing even though I set limits in Notion.
Check the Categories database really has a Number property named exactly Monthly Limit (RON), brackets and all. A differently-named or wrongly-typed property reads as empty.
A save failed with a message about a property that does not exist.
A property name in Notion does not match §4, or a Select is missing the option being written. Selects are closed lists: writing an unregistered option makes Notion reject the entire write, so the whole transaction fails rather than just that field. Currencies are the usual culprit — register all sixteen.
Why is my expense defaulting to the wrong Revolut?
If you have two accounts with the same name in different currencies, the app prefers the RON one for everyday spending. Pick the other explicitly when you mean it, and the currency follows the account.
Reminders are enabled but nothing arrives.
Check, in order: the app is installed and launched from its icon; the browser is Chromium; notification permission is granted; and a bill genuinely falls inside your lead time. Then use the diagnostics panel — seven taps on the Bill Reminders heading. Background delivery is best-effort by design and the browser may simply decline.
The app says it is showing old data.
It could not reach Notion and is showing the last copy it downloaded. Anything you change is queued and marked ⏳ Syncing, and goes out when the connection returns.
Something is flagged as a duplicate but isn't.
Press Not duplicates; it is remembered on that device. If it happens often for a fixed-price purchase, that vendor and amount probably has not appeared on enough separate days yet for the app to recognise it as a habit.
Can I edit records directly in Notion?
Yes — that is the point. The app reads whatever is there next time it loads. The one exception is a subscription's LastProcessed, which the posting engine manages; editing it by hand can cause a charge to be posted twice or skipped.
Is my token safe?
It is stored in this browser only and sent solely to Notion. Nothing is retained by any intermediary. Grant the integration access only to the five databases it needs — Notion integrations see nothing you have not shared. Revoke it any time at notion.so/my-integrations.
How do I start completely fresh?
Either delete the rows in Notion yourself, or use Erase Notion data & use samples in Settings — read the warning in §12 first.
Your money, your Notion, your device. WhereItWent stores nothing of its own.
