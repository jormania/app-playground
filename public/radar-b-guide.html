📡 Guide

Radar-B

A personal cultural radar for Bucharest.

Radar-B answers one question: what interesting things are happening in Bucharest that I might want to go to?

Today that answer is scattered across Facebook, local publications, newsletters, cultural websites, museum pages and Notion. Radar-B brings them together into a single calm stream — one event with many sources, never four copies of the same exhibition — and hands anything worth doing to Wanderlist.

1How the whole thing fits together

Radar-B is one piece of a chain that already existed. It doesn't replace any of it — it's the part where all that gathered information finally becomes usable on a phone.

sources event intelligence discovery decision ────────────────────── ───────────────────── ─────────── ────────────── B365, Curatorial, /recommend in Bucharest ┌─────────┐ ┌────────────┐ Buletin, HotNews, ──► fetch · parse · dedupe ──► │ RADAR-B │ ──► │ WANDERLIST │ Zile și Nopți, taste-filter │ browse │ save │ plan · go │ Harta Muzeelor, │ │ evaluate│ │ │ Recomandata, │ writes └─────────┘ └────────────┘ venues, Facebook ▼ Notion 🗓️ Suggested events ← the articles (unchanged) 📡 Radar ← the events themselves

The important part, in one sentence:

Radar-B does not scrape anything. Claude is the parser, Notion is the wire format, Radar-B is the client. The app reads events that /recommend in Bucharest has already fetched, parsed, deduplicated and filtered to your taste.

Why it's built that way

  1. A scraper would duplicate the skill. The skill already reads seven Romanian publications every week and applies real editorial judgement. A parallel set of HTML parsers would be a worse second copy of that, with none of the judgement.
  2. It wouldn't survive. Seven publications' markup, each free to change any week. Every source that broke would break silently, and you'd only notice by the events that quietly stopped appearing.
  3. It couldn't deploy. Vercel's Hobby plan caps this whole repo at 12 serverless functions, and it was already at exactly 12. Radar-B ships with zero new functions — everything goes through the existing /api/notion relay.

The practical consequence is worth stating plainly: Radar-B is only as fresh as your last skill run. That's a real trade-off, and the app is honest about it — every event shows when it was last checked, and the masthead shows when the week was last refreshed.

2The /recommend in Bucharest skill

This is the engine. You run it in a Claude conversation by typing /recommend in Bucharest. It does two things now: it prints the curated Romanian digest it always did, and it leaves behind structured event rows that Radar-B reads all week.

What it reads

SourceKindRhythm
Buletin de București — Agenda Urbană cu CosminArticlePublishes Thu/Fri, covers the whole week (Mon–Sun)
HotNews — Weekend trending în BucureștiArticleThu/Fri
B365 — București de weekendArticleThu or earlier

Harta Muzeelor / Weekend SessionsAggregatorAlways current — museums, galleries, tours, workshops

Plus, per event, a real search for that event's own page — the venue, gallery, or ticket platform (iaBilet, Eventbook, Bilet.ro). A roundup article is a fallback, not the target.

Two modes, by day of week

  • Friday–Sunday (weekend mode). Notion is the source of truth. If the 🗓️ Suggested events page already covers this weekend it's used as-is; otherwise the skill refreshes all sources first. On Friday and Saturday it also re-checks any ⏳ nepublicat încă placeholders, since Buletin and HotNews often post late.
  • Monday–Thursday (weekday mode). Notion and live web search, always combined — the articles already linked in Notion are parsed for weekday events too, not just weekend ones.

What it filters for

The skill knows your taste and applies it before anything reaches Radar-B.

Always includedAlways excluded
Contemporary & experimental art (the core) · arthouse cinema (Elvire Popesco, Europa, Apollo, MȚR, Cinema Pro, open-air) · jazz & improvisation · contemporary/chamber music · indie, alternative, folk · performance in hybrid spaces (Control, Guesthouse, Cărturești, galleries) · book launches and literary events · cultural & social conferences (art, urbanism, design, philosophy) · street fairs with cultural character · guided tours and heritage walks · newly opened venues · everything at the Atheneum Sala Palatului · Teatrul Nottara · MINA · Teatrul Bulandra · Opera Națională · Opereta · Tur Pietonal · FF Theatre · The Fool · corporate events, trade fairs and business conferences · mainstream arena pop unless genuinely exceptional

Price is never a filter. Nothing is excluded for being expensive, and cost is never flagged as a reason to skip something.

The governing instinct: fringe, textural, non-obvious. Given a safe mainstream pick and a smaller interesting one, the skill takes the smaller interesting one. Specificity over completeness. A handful of broad-appeal events still make it, grouped at the bottom of the digest under „De știut" so they're easy to skip.

What you get back

A day-by-day digest, 12–20 events, numbered sequentially, each with venue, full street address, exact time where known, 2–3 sentences of context, a Wanderlist category, price in lei if stated, and a source link. Flags: 💚 free · 📋 booking needed · 🔁 recurring · 📍 new venue. There's a Pe radar 👁️ section for things further out that are worth saving now.

And then — Step 4b

After the digest, the skill writes that same deduplicated pool into the 📡 Radar Notion database: one row per underlying event, one Sources line per mention. That's what Radar-B reads. It updates rows in place by their Key rather than duplicating them, and never deletes past events.

The two systems reinforce each other. The skill gives you judgement on demand; Radar-B gives you the universe, browsable at a bus stop. From any view in the app, „Întreabă Recommend in Bucharest despre astea" copies the current list as a paste-ready brief — so questions like "which of these would I enjoy this weekend?" or "what should I do with Nora?" start from the real current pool instead of a fresh round of searching.

3Using Radar-B day to day

Open it, browse for two minutes, find something, save it, leave. That's the whole intended shape. It's a PWA — install it from the Cabinet or straight from the page.

Lenses, not a calendar

The row across the top is how you move through time. Each one carries a count.

LensWhat it shows
AziEverything happening today. The default.
MâineTomorrow.
WeekendFriday–Sunday. During the weekend this means this one, not next.
SăptămânaThe rest of this calendar week, ending Sunday — not a rolling seven days, so next weekend correctly falls under “Mai încolo”.
Mai încoloBeyond that — things worth planning ahead for.
În cursExhibitions and long runs open right now. A date-first view buries these; they appear on their opening day and never again. This is where they live.
NoiEvents that arrived in the last refresh that you haven't opened yet. The answer to "what haven't I heard about?"

An event can answer to several lenses at once — a festival running Friday to Sunday is in Azi on Friday and in Weekend. Forcing one bucket is how calendars lose things.

Reading a card

When · name · where · two lines of why · at most two badges. That's deliberate: a card with no badge is completely normal, not a failure. The signals, in priority order:

recomandat gratuit cu copii loc nou în aer liber se vede oricând recurent bilet sold out

A recommended event also gets a single amber hairline down its left edge, and floats to the top of its own day — never jumping days, so the stream stays chronologically legible. N surse means several independent sources mentioned it, which is itself a confidence signal. în wanderlist means it's already saved.

Day groups and „Oricând"

The stream groups by day. Long runs and undated things are lifted out into a separate Oricând section at the bottom — an exhibition open for four months doesn't belong under "Saturday".

Search and filters

The magnifier searches title, venue, neighbourhood, address, organizer, summary, category and source name — so "what did Curatorial recommend?" is a real query, and only this app can answer it. Diacritics are ignored in both directions: improvizatie finds improvizație. Every term must match, so jazz control means both words, not either.

The filter sheet offers category, neighbourhood and signal chips — counted over what's actually in the pool, so a filter that would return nothing is never offered.

Search is for what you can name. The lenses are for what you can't — Google is good at questions you already know how to ask; this is meant to be good at showing you things you didn't know existed.

4The event page

Tapping a card answers, in this order:

  1. What is this? — poster, name, and a plain warning if the information is uncertain
  2. When? — in prose ("azi, 21:00", "până pe 14 septembrie"). If the time isn't known it says ora neconfirmată rather than inventing one
  3. Where? — venue, street address, neighbourhood, and Deschide în Maps
  4. How much? — Gratuit, a figure in lei, or an honest Preț necunoscut
  5. Why might I care? — the editorial summary, plus who recommended it
  6. How do I go? — Bilete or Pagina evenimentului
  7. Where did this come from? — De unde știm, every source with its date and link
  8. Can I save it? — Salvează în Wanderlist

Google Maps

The Maps link is built as venue, street, București — never a bare venue name, which often doesn't drop a pin. The same URL is written into Wanderlist's Map property when you save, so a saved event arrives already pinned.

Ascunde (dismissing)

The event page's top bar has two labelled controls: Înapoi returns to the list, Ascunde removes the event from the stream. They used to be a bare chevron and a bare minus, which gave no clue which was which.

Hiding syncs across your devices. It writes a Dismissed checkbox on the event's Radar row, so something hidden on your phone is hidden on your laptop too — it isn't a per-browser note. A toast offers Anulează immediately after, and Settings → Ce intră în Radar can show hidden events again at any time. Nothing is deleted; the Radar row stays exactly where it was.

Dismissal is the app's column, not the skill's. Dismissed and Dismissed At are the only two fields Radar-B ever writes on a Radar row, and /recommend in Bucharest is instructed never to touch them — otherwise a weekly refresh would resurrect everything you'd deliberately hidden.

5Dedupe, provenance and trust

If the same exhibition appears in B365, Facebook, Curatorial and the Notion recommendations, you should see one event with four sources, not four events. That happens in two passes, on purpose.

  • Upstream, in the skill — semantic. Claude can read two articles and know they mean the same exhibition. It writes one row with a merged Sources list.
  • Downstream, in the app — structural. The same event arriving across different refresh runs, and from Radar and Wanderlist at once. Deterministic and heavily tested.

How the app decides two things are one

  1. An identical Key is always the same event.
  2. Otherwise: normalised title similarity (diacritics folded in both encodings — ș and ş — Romanian filler words dropped), venue similarity, and date overlap.
  3. Title similarity is a necessary condition. Venue and date can only ever confirm a match, never create one — so "Jazz in the Park" and "Street Delivery" at the same venue on the same night stay two events.
  4. When in doubt, keep separate. Two cards for one event is a mild annoyance. One card that swallowed a different event hides something real.

Merging keeps provenance instead of flattening it

Each field is taken from the highest-confidence source that has it (confirmed > reported > uncertain, ties broken by whichever was checked most recently), and the union of sources and signals is kept. A merge can never lose recommended, and never loses a mention.

One detail worth knowing: date and time move together. A precise time is only ever taken from the same source that supplied the date, so a merge can't invent a time on the wrong day.

Freshness and confidence

StateWhat it meansHow it looks
confirmedRead off the event's own pageNormal
reportedFrom an editorial roundupNormal
uncertainJust a title and a date inside an articleDimmed, marked aproximativ, and a full sentence on the event page telling you to verify before leaving the house

Anything not re-checked in 14 days is marked stale. "Never checked" is shown as its own state — it never silently reads as "fresh".

6Saving to Wanderlist

Discover → evaluate → save → do.

Radar-B handles the first two. Wanderlist handles the things you decide to keep. There is no favourites list, no stars, no bookmarks in Radar-B — a parallel store would just be a second place to lose things. Salvează în Wanderlist writes one row into your Findings database, and from then on the event lives there.

Nothing is written without you seeing it first

Save opens an editable draft, pre-filled from the event. You can change every field before anything is created.

Findings fieldFilled from
NameThe event name, without the venue
DescriptionThe editorial summary. Never blank — a thin source gets the shortest honest sentence plus "de verificat", rather than an empty field
LinkTicket link first, then the event's own page, then the article that mentioned it
CategoryWanderlist's own closed vocabulary, verbatim — falls back to event rather than inventing a value
PlaceVenue + street + București — the full address, so the map pin lands first try
MapThe Google Maps search URL for that address
CostPrice in lei, if stated. Never set alongside free
TagsOnly signals that map to Wanderlist's tag vocabulary (free, ticketed, outdoor). Radar-B's own concepts never leak in
Date ExpiringThe event's date for a one-off, the closing date for a run, blank for anything recurring or undated — no deadline is ever invented
Planned DateThe event's date, with a time only if a real one is known
Date AddedToday
Attended / GoingAlways false. A known date is not a commitment — you decide that in Wanderlist

One schema, two apps. Radar-B writes through literally the same function Wanderlist writes with (src/shared/findings.js). A row saved from Radar-B and one saved in Wanderlist are identical by construction, not by careful copying — they can't drift apart into one writing "Free" and the other "free".

What Wanderlist tells Radar-B back

The link runs both ways. For anything already in Findings, the event page shows a compact În Wanderlist row carrying the decisions you already made there — Mergi / Încă nedecis (the Going checkbox), the planned date and time, whether you're holding tickets, and the expiry — plus a direct link into the Wanderlist entry. The basics are answerable without leaving Radar-B.

Three Findings states also control what appears at all, as toggles under Settings → Ce intră în Radar (all on by default):

Hidden by defaultWhy
Attended = yesYou've been. It is not a thing to go to.
IdeasWanderlist's someday/maybe: no planned date and no deadline. A loose intention, not an event with a time.
venue · idea · discoveryPlaces and tips rather than happenings — a café worth visiting has no “when”.

Each toggle reports how many events it is currently hiding, so a thin-looking week is never a mystery — and switching one off brings them straight back.

What Radar-B sends the other way

A save carries its provenance with it. Because Findings has no Sources field, Radar-B appends one line to the description — “📡 Via Radar-B — recomandat de Curatorial; menționat de B365.” — so the context that made the event worth saving survives the handoff instead of being dropped at the boundary.

Already saved

Radar-B reads your Findings database too, and folds it into the same dedupe pool. So anything already on your Wanderlist shows as în wanderlist and is never offered for saving twice — even when the Wanderlist entry is worded differently from the Radar row.

7Sources, and Facebook specifically

Every source arrives the same way — as a line in an event's Sources field. None is privileged. On the event page each one is labelled: recomandare when that source actively recommended it, menționat for a passing reference, wanderlist for your own saved copy.

Facebook

Facebook stays important — plenty of Bucharest events are hard to find anywhere else. But you get the events, not Facebook.

  • No scraper, no Graph API, no paid data vendor. As of 2026 the Graph API covers almost none of public Facebook, public event pages are served to logged-out browsers only as JSON buried in script tags behind aggressive anti-automation, and the third-party unified APIs are all paid and brittle.
  • Facebook events enter through the skill, like everything else — Claude reads a public event (or a screenshot, which is what actually works reliably) and writes a normal Radar row.
  • In the app a Facebook-origin event is rendered fully natively: title, time, venue, summary, poster. The Facebook URL appears exactly once, in the Sources list, as an escape hatch.
  • Radar-B never embeds Facebook, never links to a feed, and never asks you to log in.

Adding a source later

Because sources are just named lines rather than code, adding one is a change to the skill, not to the app. Radar-B needs no update to display a source it has never seen before.

8Setup

Try it with nothing at all

With no Notion token, Radar-B runs in demo mode on a full plausible Bucharest week — including a duplicate exhibition that visibly merges, an uncertain event, a long run, a festival and an already-saved item. Nothing to configure.

Connecting your own Notion

  1. Open Setări (the gear in the masthead).
  2. Paste your Notion integration token. It stays in this browser only — it's relayed per request through /api/notion, which stores nothing.
  3. The three ids below it are already filled in with the right defaults. Each accepts a pasted Notion URL or a bare id.
  4. Testează checks reachability, then Salvează.
SettingWhat it's forIf it's missing
Baza RadarThe 📡 Radar database — the events themselvesThe app still works off Wanderlist and the Suggested page
Baza FindingsWanderlist. Where saving writes, and how "already saved" is detectedNo save, no saved-state detection
Pagina Suggested eventsRead only to show which articles this week was built fromThat one section disappears

Your Notion integration must be shared with all three. In Notion, open each database or page → ⋯ → Connections → add your integration. A token alone isn't enough; Notion grants access per page.

Filling Radar with real events

📡 Radar starts empty. It was deliberately not seeded — invented events would have made the app look alive while lying about the city. Run /recommend in Bucharest once and it fills.

Offline

The last successful fetch is cached, so opening the app on the metro shows the last known week rather than an empty screen. Saving to Wanderlist needs a connection and says so rather than queueing silently.

You don't need this to use the app — it's here so the skill and the database can be kept in step, and so you can edit a row by hand in Notion when you want to.

Radar-B never writes to Radar. The skill owns that table. Two writers to one table is how a working Notion setup gets damaged by accident. The app's only write, anywhere, is one Findings row when you tap Save.

10What it deliberately doesn't do

Not builtWhy
Scrapers / per-source fetchersSection 1. The skill does this better, and the function budget is full
A map viewNeighbourhood chips and a Maps link do the work. Wanderlist learned this one the expensive way
NotificationsWanderlist already emails you the evening before something expires. A second nagging channel is exactly the thing this app is meant not to be
A favourites / bookmarks storeWanderlist is the home for saved things. Section 6

11Troubleshooting

The stream is empty on every lens

Most likely 📡 Radar hasn't been filled yet. Run /recommend in Bucharest. If you've already run it, check Setări → Testează, and confirm your Notion integration is shared with the Radar database.

"Liniște" on one lens but not others

Normal. There genuinely may be nothing tonight. Try Weekend or În desfășurare — the sources publish Thursday and Friday, so early in the week is naturally thin.

Two cards for what's obviously one event

The dedupe is deliberately cautious. Give the two rows the same Key in Notion and they'll merge on the next load.

An event shows the wrong time

Check the event page's De unde știm — it names which source supplied what and when it was last checked. Fix it in the Radar row and set Confidence to confirmed.

Saving fails

Saving needs a live connection. If it's online, the error text comes straight from Notion — usually the integration isn't shared with the Findings database, or a Category value doesn't exist there yet.

An event I saved still isn't marked

The app refetches on load and when you return to the tab. Pull up and reopen, or switch away and back.
